1. Executive Summary & Problem Context

Financial fraud across global digital payment rails is an adversarial, multi-billion-dollar challenge. In modern high-volume payment processing, fraud does not present as an obvious outlier; it is hidden within millions of legitimate card activities, typically accounting for less than 0.2% of total transactional volume.

This creates the classic Banking Risk Dilemma: if a financial institution configures fraud detection rules too aggressively, innocent cardholders suffer false declines, causing customer cart abandonment, public embarrassment, and irreversible account churn. Conversely, if fraud screening thresholds are set too loosely, the institution hemorrhages tens of thousands of dollars in unrecoverable chargebacks and regulatory fines.

Project Breakthrough: Our 3-member team engineered an integrated risk platform that resolves this trade-off. By pairing an audited relational data pipeline with a balanced Random Forest classifier, we achieved an 81.63% fraud catch rate ($49,082 direct loss prevented) while eliminating 98.3% of investigator false alarms (reducing false positive alerts from 1,492 down to 26). This generates +$47,782 in net economic value on the test partition and saves 366.5 hours of manual fraud investigation overhead.

The Two Behavioral Phenomena Discovered

1. The Bi-Modal Modus Operandi: Fraud syndicates operate in two distinct phases:
  • Phase 1: Automated Micro Card-Testing (< $5): Criminals use botnets to execute 27 transactions between $0 and $5 to test card validity without triggering cardholder alerts.
  • Phase 2: High-Velocity Fund Extraction ($500 to $2,000): Once confirmed, criminals execute high-ticket purchases (18 transactions > $500, driving 40.2% of all fraud losses) up to $2,125.87.
2. The 2:00 AM Vulnerability Spike: Between 00:00 and 05:00, legitimate cardholder traffic plummets by 85%. Cybercriminals deliberately time high-value transactions during these hours because push notifications and SMS alerts go unread, providing an average 6-to-8 hour latency window before the victim awakens.

6. Senior Q&A Defense Matrix (Tough Questions & Answers)

Senior Question Speaker Data-Backed Defense Answer
"Why not block all transactions at 2:00 AM if fraud surges 10x?" Yash "Over 85% of 2:00 AM traffic is still legitimate (international travelers, ride-shares, shift workers). Blunt blocking causes massive customer churn. Instead, our feature pipeline triggers adaptive Step-Up MFA specifically for nighttime transactions over $150."
"Why choose Random Forest over Logistic Regression if LR caught 9 more frauds?" Anjali "Catching those 9 extra frauds with Logistic Regression required 1,466 additional false alarms (1,492 vs 26), burning 366 analyst hours and yielding a negative ROI (-$19.9k). Random Forest achieves 75.5% precision with a 0.3:1 false alarm ratio, yielding $47.8k in net economic value."
"Why did you use PR-AUC instead of standard ROC-AUC?" Anjali "In extreme imbalance (0.173%), ROC-AUC is distorted by hundreds of thousands of True Negatives, making mediocre models look deceptively good (0.97). PR-AUC focuses strictly on the minority fraud class, where Random Forest achieved a dominant 0.8433 vs 0.7225."
"How is data structured inside Power BI for fast query response?" Aditya "We avoided loading unindexed flat CSVs. We architected an analytical star-schema with dedicated dimension tables for Time, Amount Bands, and Risk Tiers, pre-calculating KPIs with 35+ modular DAX measures so slicers update instantaneously."
"How does the bank handle customer friction in Tier 2?" Aditya "Tier 2 uses Step-Up MFA (biometrics / push notification) rather than an outright decline. Legitimate customers verify in 10 seconds, while automated fraud bots without the physical device are immediately stopped."
"How was data leakage prevented during feature engineering?" Yash "All scalers and transformers were packaged inside Scikit-Learn pipelines and fitted strictly on the training partition. No test set records were ever exposed during preprocessing or scaling."
